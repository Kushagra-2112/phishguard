the pipeline
Four stages, one decision.

A phishing email rarely fails in one obvious way — the writing can sound completely legitimate while the link underneath it points somewhere it shouldn't, or vice versa. PhishGuard encodes each signal on its own terms before deciding how much to trust it.

01 · TEXT
Email → ModernBERT
Reads the full message body for the fingerprints of social engineering — manufactured urgency, spoofed sender tone, threats of account loss, requests that skip normal verification.
→
02 · URL
URL → CANINE
Reads the link one character at a time — catching a swapped digit, an extra hyphen, or a lookalike domain that a subword tokenizer would silently merge into "normal."
→
03 · FUSE
Gated fusion
A small learned network looks at both embeddings and decides, per message, how much weight the text deserves versus the link — never a fixed 50/50 split.
→
04 · DECIDE
Verdict
A binary call — phishing or legitimate — returned alongside the exact gate weights, so the reasoning behind the call isn't a black box.
what it's looking for
The tells attackers can't fully hide.

Four patterns that show up again and again in real phishing campaigns — each one is exactly why the architecture is split the way it is.

01 · TYPOSQUATTING
PHISH arnaz0n-support.com
REAL amazon.com
A single swapped character is enough to fool a skim-reader. CANINE reads at the character level specifically because this trick is invisible to word-based models.
02 · IP-BASED HOSTS
PHISH http://185.63.20.14/login
REAL https://accounts.google.com
Legitimate services almost never ask you to log in at a bare IP address. It's a cheap, disposable way to stand up infrastructure with no domain to trace.
03 · MANUFACTURED URGENCY
PHISH "Verify now or lose access in 24h"
REAL "Your statement is ready to view"
Urgency is designed to short-circuit careful reading. ModernBERT is trained on exactly this kind of pressure language, not just keyword matching.
04 · MISMATCHED SIGNALS
PHISH Calm, professional email → sketchy link
REAL Consistent tone and destination
The most convincing attacks get the writing right and hide behind a bad link — or the reverse. This is the exact case fusion exists to catch.
why fusion, not one model
Neither signal alone is enough.

A model trained only on text learns to spot bad writing — and misses a clean, well-written email hiding a malicious link. A model trained only on URLs never sees the social-engineering half of the attack at all. Fusion exists because most real phishing lives in the gap between the two.

▲
Handles missing data
Most real-world samples arrive with only a URL or only a body of text — rarely both. A learned "absent" embedding lets each branch reason confidently even when its input is empty, instead of guessing from noise.
◆
Character-aware URLs
micros0ft-support.com and microsoft.com tokenize almost identically under a word-piece vocabulary. CANINE reads raw characters, so the swapped digit is visible instead of averaged away.
●
Explainable weighting
Every verdict ships with the fusion gate's exact weights — so "94% phishing, driven mostly by the URL" is a real, inspectable statement, not a black-box score.
